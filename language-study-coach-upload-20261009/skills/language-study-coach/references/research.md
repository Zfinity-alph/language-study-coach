# Focused verification

Browse when comprehensive investigation, unstable terminology, disputed usage, frequency, modern morphology, root identity, affix meaning, or etymology requires evidence. Routine stable explanations evident from an exercise need not become research essays.

Prefer authoritative dictionaries, academic grammars, language institutes, reputable corpora, university resources, and official exam criteria. For Russian, Gramota dictionary resources and the Russian National Corpus may help when they address the claim. Search in the target language. Treat forums and machine-generated summaries as leads, not decisive evidence.

Open and inspect the supporting source. Cite direct page links near claims, not search-result pages. Distinguish prescriptions, observed usage, historical accounts, and tutor inference. Check more than one independent source for disputed claims. Stop when the important decision and exceptions are supported.

If browsing or a relevant source is unavailable, state the limitation. Do not manufacture citations, certainty, frequency, CEFR precision, or derivational relations. Continue reliable portions and leave the uncertain claim unresolved.
